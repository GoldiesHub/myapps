Module 1 · Week 1 · Lesson 1

Understanding Strategy: How Organisations Choose to Win

Learning purpose: Develop a disciplined understanding of strategy as a set of choices about where and how an organisation will compete, create value and sustain advantage.

1. What strategy is

Strategy is not a slogan, a vision statement or a long list of activities. It is an integrated set of choices that responds to a significant challenge and establishes a coherent path to success.

Richard Rumelt describes good strategy through three connected elements: a diagnosis of the challenge, a guiding policy for addressing it, and coherent actions that reinforce one another.

2. Strategy versus related concepts

  • Vision: the future an organisation aspires to create.
  • Mission: its fundamental purpose and reason for existing.
  • Objectives: the outcomes it intends to achieve.
  • Plans: the activities, resources and timelines used to execute.
  • Strategy: the choices and logic connecting the organisation's position to its desired outcomes.

3. Choice, focus and trade-offs

A strategy requires choices. An organisation cannot serve every customer, pursue every opportunity or be excellent at everything. Competitive positioning involves deciding what to do, what not to do, and how activities reinforce the chosen position.

4. Competitive advantage

Advantage may arise from cost, differentiation, access, trust, network effects, proprietary capabilities, scale, distribution or regulatory permissions. The strategic question is whether the advantage is valuable, difficult to imitate and likely to endure.

5. Why strategies fail

  • The diagnosis misunderstands the real challenge.
  • Ambitions are mistaken for choices.
  • Initiatives are disconnected and compete for resources.
  • Critical assumptions are not tested.
  • External changes invalidate the business model.
  • Execution capabilities are weaker than the strategy requires.

6. The risk leader's lens

Before identifying risks, understand what the organisation is trying to achieve and what must be true for its strategy to work. Ask: What assumptions underpin growth? Which customers, markets, partners, technologies or regulatory conditions are essential? What could make the economics stop working?

Reflection exercise

Choose a familiar organisation. In no more than 250 words, explain its strategic choice, how it creates an advantage, and one assumption that could invalidate that advantage.

Recommended study

  • Richard Rumelt, Good Strategy/Bad Strategy, Chapters 1–5.
  • Michael Porter, “What Is Strategy?”, Harvard Business Review, 1996.
  • Explore the organisation's annual report and strategy statement.
